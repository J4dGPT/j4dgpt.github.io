Clypio bills brands only for views that survive verification.

Creators cut a brand's long-form video into shorts and post them from their own accounts. The brand funds a pool and pays per thousand views, but only for views that survive verification. Clypio keeps the spread between the brand rate and the creator rate.

I designed and built the whole system: the FastAPI engine, the verification and fraud scoring package, the money model, the operator, brand and creator dashboards, and the marketing site. The engine runs on Render with Postgres, and the site is on Vercel.

No overspend under concurrent approvals

The budget gate runs inside a per-campaign lock. A smoke test fires ten simultaneous approvals at a budget that covers three clips and expects exactly three to pass. The lock is in-process, so the engine reads WEB_CONCURRENCY at boot and refuses to start with more than one worker until the lock moves to Postgres.

A billing bug copied into six places

Tier logic summed raw view counts instead of verified ones. The two are equal until a clip hits its cap, after which a capped, barely paid clip could push a creator into a higher tier and lift their cap. I replaced all six copies with one function, lifetime_billable_views(), and added tests that pin the verified basis.

Schema changes without downtime

Production schema is owned by hand-written Alembic migrations that Render runs as a pre-deploy step, before the new instance takes traffic. An early autogenerated migration added a non-null column with no default and failed on existing rows, which is why migrations are now written and reviewed by hand.
